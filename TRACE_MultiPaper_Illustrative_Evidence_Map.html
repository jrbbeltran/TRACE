What these cases test

Multiple operational outcomes; competing and null hypotheses; experimental mediation; nonlinear moderation; CEO panel analysis; citation existence; exact table and page locations. Claim mappings reflect what the authors report, without validating their data or models.

Journal of Business Venturing

The “devil” you don’t know: A test of the detriments and benefits of co-founding with strangers

Greenberg & Mollick (2026) · DOI

Kickstarter founder survey (3,562 ventures); OLS, logistic/ordered/fractional models; coarsened exact matching

A. Theoretical claims

THEORETICAL CLAIM

Co-founders who were previously strangers may add nonredundant networks, but uncertainty about trust and coordination creates operational costs.

Author support: Burt (2004) and Stinchcombe (1965) among cited supports

Locate: PDF pp. 2–3; “Stranger Puzzle” theory, H1 and H2

External record check: Burt (2004): publisher bibliographic record located (DOI 10.1086/421787); existence only.

B. Empirical claims

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

Teams including strangers attract more external backers.

Reported support: Authors report approximately 22% more backers after controls (reported b = 0.1972, p = .045).

Locate: PDF p. 10, results text; Table 4, PDF p. 11

Reported association; not an independent calculation or causal assessment.

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

The presumed skills advantage does not appear in the reported models.

Reported support: Authors report no significant differences in breadth/depth of team skills, including the matched-model comparison.

Locate: PDF p. 10, results text; Table 5, PDF pp. 13–14

Negative or null findings belong in the map alongside positive claims.

Journal of Applied Psychology

Setting Higher Referral Targets Increases the Number of Women Recommended: Evidence From the Field and Lab

Rai, Kirgios, Lucas, & Milkman (2026) · DOI

Field and online experiments plus supplementary studies; OLS and mediation; N = 12,615 overall

A. Theoretical claims

THEORETICAL CLAIM

Higher referral targets can motivate people to generate more referrals, increasing the number of women recommended.

Author support: Locke & Latham (2002) and goal-setting literature

Locate: PDF pp. 2–3; goal-setting account and H1/H2

External record check: Locke & Latham (2002): APA/PubMed bibliographic record located (DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705); existence only.

B. Empirical claims

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

Requesting more referrals increases the number of women referred in Study 1.

Reported support: Authors report 17% more women referred (mean .30 vs .26; b = .06, p = .03).

Locate: PDF p. 7 (journal p. 1511), hypothesis tests; Table 1 and Figure 2

Source pointers aid retrieval; they do not validate the design.

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

Prototype-divergence prediction is not consistently supported.

Reported support: The field-study analysis reports an insignificant effect on the likelihood that a given referral is a woman (b = −.002, p = .90), with mixed evidence across settings.

Locate: PDF p. 8 (journal p. 1512), Study 1 results; abstract, PDF p. 1

TRACE should preserve this qualification rather than flatten it into a blanket positive claim.

Personnel Psychology

Voluntary Turnover Rate Fluctuations, Human Resource Practices, and Innovation: A Within-Organization Investigation

Li, Huang, Dineen, Wang, & van Jaarsveld (2025; online 2024) · DOI

Statistics Canada panel; 7,110 workplace-year observations across 1,980 workplaces; firm fixed-effects, quadratic terms and moderations

A. Theoretical claims

THEORETICAL CLAIM

Complex adaptive systems reasoning predicts a U-shaped within-organization turnover–innovation relationship and shifts in its threshold due to HR practices.

Author support: Nyberg & Ployhart (2013), Dooley (1997), and related theory

Locate: PDF pp. 2–5 (journal pp. 104–107), theory, H1–H4

External record check: Nyberg & Ployhart (2013): Academy of Management Review record located (DOI 10.5465/amr.2011.0201); existence only.

B. Empirical claims

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

A U-shaped within-workplace relationship appears in the main models.

Reported support: Authors report linear B = −.38 and quadratic B = .88 (both p < .001) and state H1 is supported.

Locate: PDF p. 7 (journal p. 109), results; Table 2 (PDF pp. 9–10)

Model coefficients are copied from author reporting, not re-estimated.

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

HR practices shift the relationship’s estimated threshold.

Reported support: Authors interpret employee participation and group-based pay as lowering the threshold, with individual-based pay raising it.

Locate: PDF p. 11 (journal p. 113), moderation results; Figures 2–4

Evidence depends on interactions/curves, not a single coefficient or p-value.

Strategic Management Journal

Do boards learn to hire? The effect of board experience with CEO replacement on CEO performance

Boivie, Gee, Gentry, & Graffin (2025) · DOI

Longitudinal director/firm experience measures; 1,345 CEO successions in 900 firms; regression and robustness analyses

A. Theoretical claims

THEORETICAL CLAIM

Rare, complex CEO hiring decisions can create superstitious learning instead of reliably improved selection.

Author support: Zollo (2009) and organizational-learning literature

Locate: PDF pp. 3–6 (journal pp. 2469–2472), theory and H1

External record check: Zollo (2009): INFORMS bibliographic record located (DOI 10.1287/orsc.1090.0459); existence only.

B. Empirical claims

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

More previous CEO-selection experience is associated with lower subsequent CEO performance.

Reported support: Authors report b = −1.18, p = .01 in their main model and indicate support for H1.

Locate: PDF p. 13 (journal p. 2479), results; Table 3 (PDF pp. 16–17)

Reported CiC-performance association; not an independent quality assessment.

EMPIRICAL CLAIM

Domain-specific CEO hiring experience does not consistently offset the negative association.

Reported support: Authors report H2a/H2b unsupported; reported strategic-similarity interactions b = −.43 (p = .29) and b = .22 (p = .57).

Locate: PDF p. 13 (journal p. 2479), H2 results; Table 3 (PDF pp. 16–17)

Preserves failure to support proposed boundary conditions.

Important boundaries

This is a source-navigation illustration, not an automated scientific audit. It contains selected claims, not an exhaustive inventory. Manuscripts require holistic review and direct inspection by human experts. A matched publication record does not establish that the cited article substantively supports its attribution.
