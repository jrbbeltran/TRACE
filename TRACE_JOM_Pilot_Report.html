Manuscript overview

Research question. How do organizations contribute to the perpetuation of means–ends decoupling under heightened field scrutiny?

Design. Inductive, qualitative theory-building study based primarily on 75 interviews with representatives of 68 organizations in fashion supply chains in India and Bangladesh.

Claimed contribution. Auditability-based goal replacement: organizations present compliance as evidence of commitment while orienting internal goal pursuit toward auditable thresholds. The authors theorize self-silencing and instrumentalizing as reinforcing dynamics.

Structure. Theoretical framing pp. 2–7, method pp. 7–14, findings pp. 14–24, theoretical framework Figure 2 (p. 24), discussion pp. 25–31, references pp. 32–36.

What this report does not establish

It does not validate interview data, coding decisions, alternative explanations, causal claims, the novelty of the mechanism, or the overall scientific quality of the paper. Four checked references are a selected sample and are not a comprehensive audit of the bibliography. All substantive judgments belong to human experts.

T1. Means–ends decoupling differs from policy–practice decoupling

Theoretical

The authors use MED to describe circumstances in which organizations implement prescribed practices but do not achieve their intended societal goals.

T2. Heightened scrutiny can pressure organizations toward substantive action

Empirical and interpretation claims

In this qualitative paper, evidence is interview excerpts, coding structure, and interpretive analysis—not regression tables.

E1. Compliance is used rhetorically as evidence of commitment

Empirical

The authors interpret actors’ descriptions of compliance, duty, professionalization, and worker well-being as a rhetoric in which visible compliance signals commitment to the field goal.

Author's wording · p. 14

“compliance is offered as supposed proof of that commitment”

Evidence as presented by the authors

Interview-based illustrations: a sustainability trainer equates social sustainability with compliance (TP04), while a supplier treats satisfactory audit closure as the end of the matter (S20). Figure 1 on p. 12 situates these findings within the coding structure.

E3. Overlapping audits may consume resources that could benefit workers

E5. Self-silencing and instrumentalizing can reinforce goal replacement

Empirical

The authors propose that actors may privately recognize compliance limitations yet avoid public criticism, while continuing shortfalls are used to justify more compliance.

Author's wording · p. 22

“Self-silencing thus withholds the critique that might otherwise prompt course correction”

Evidence as presented by the authors

An auditor discusses brand image, a manager describes remaining quiet for business reasons, and a supplier describes compliance as necessary to avoid worker harm. The theorized feedback relationships appear in Figure 2, p. 24.
